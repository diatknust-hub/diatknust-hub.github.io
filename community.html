About the Department

DIAT Explained

📅 July 2026 🏫 KNUST, Kumasi, Ghana 🎉 DIAT@50 Preparations

What exactly is DIAT? In under four minutes, this short film answers that question — taking you through the Department of Indigenous Art and Technology at KNUST, its six craft disciplines, its CAD-to-craft philosophy, and the unique position it holds as the only university department in Ghana dedicated entirely to indigenous craft as living, professionally relevant practice.

Founded in 1976 as the Institute of Rural Art and Industry, DIAT has spent five decades proving that Clay, Fibres & Fabrics, Leather Technology, Metal Production, Rattan & Bamboo, and Wood & Furniture are not relics — they are disciplines with real market demand, design intelligence, and cultural weight. This film is part of DIAT’s 50th Anniversary preparations.

Visits & Exchanges

When Leather Met Colour: A Sankofa Exchange at DIAT, KNUST

📅 June 2026 🏛 Leather Technology Section 🤝 Second Annual Visit

Under the spirit of Sankofa — the Ghanaian Adinkra symbol that teaches it is no fault to return to the past and carry forward what is valuable — a group of international visitors from Sacramento State joined DIAT’s Leather Technology section at KNUST for a second year running.

The day brought vegetable-tanned leather preparation, dye techniques borrowed from Fibres & Fabrics — tie-dye, marbling, knotting, and spray stencilling through lace — and ended with visitors pressing wet, freshly dyed leather over carved wooden masks to lift every detail of the carving, turning flat painted leathers into dimensional, wearable art.

DIAT remains one of the few institutions in Ghana still teaching indigenous craft as living, professionally relevant practice — adding technology to tradition rather than replacing it.

This visit is also part of DIAT’s 50th Anniversary preparations as the department marks five decades of indigenous craft education at KNUST.

The Partnership

The Mastercard Foundation Partnership

In partnership with the Mastercard Foundation, DIAT operates Ghana’s most comprehensive indigenous craft internship programme. Year 3 students spend three months embedded with artisan communities across all regions of Ghana.

The internship is not observational — students actively contribute to production, document techniques, and formally attribute the knowledge systems of their host artisan communities in reports that become part of DIAT’s permanent archive.

Programme At a Glance

→Duration: 3 months (end of Year 3)
→Coverage: Nationwide across Ghana
→Partner: Mastercard Foundation
→Output: Formal craft knowledge attribution reports
→Disciplines: All 6 craft sections
The Process

How the Internship Works

01

Community Placement

Students are placed with artisan communities across Ghana’s regions matching their craft discipline specialisation.

02

Active Production

Students contribute directly to artisan production — not as observers but as working members of the craft community.

03

Knowledge Documentation

Each student formally documents the craft knowledge systems encountered, creating attribution reports for DIAT’s archive.
