Third Arm of DIAT

Community & Internship Programme

DIAT's academic work does not end in the studio. Through a structured three-month internship programme supported by the Mastercard Foundation, students are embedded with artisan communities across Ghana — learning, documenting, and formally attributing the indigenous craft knowledge that sustains the department.

The Internship Programme

How It Works

The three-month community internship is a compulsory component of the DIAT academic programme, undertaken by Year 3 students. It is designed to ground theoretical and studio-based learning in living indigenous practice — ensuring that DIAT graduates understand their disciplines not only as academic subjects but as active, community-embedded cultural traditions.

1

Placement Matching

Students are matched with artisan communities across Ghana based on their chosen craft disciplines. The DIAT Community Liaison coordinates placements in consultation with community elders and craft leaders.

2

Embedded Practice

For three months, students work alongside community artisans, learning traditional techniques that are not formally documented anywhere else. They observe, participate in, and document production processes from raw material to finished piece.

3

Documentation & Attribution

Students formally document the techniques, knowledge, and innovations they encounter. All documentation is reviewed by the department and credited to the artisan community — creating a growing record of indigenous craft knowledge with verified attribution.

4

Reflection & Exhibition

Students return to KNUST with field documentation, materials, and a community-inspired project. These projects are presented at the annual DIAT Community Exhibition, giving artisan communities visibility within the university and public contexts.
