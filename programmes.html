What We Teach

Six Academic Programmes

Every DIAT student begins with Product Design — learning to think, draw, and digitally prototype before committing to craft. Then they specialise in two of six indigenous disciplines, graduating as both cultural custodians and design professionals.

Foundational Programme

Built on Product Design Thinking

Before any student touches clay, metal, leather, or wood at DIAT, they spend dedicated time in Product Design — the foundational strand that runs across all year groups from Year 1 through to final year. This is not a separate discipline but the intellectual and technical spine of the entire DIAT curriculum.

📐
Foundational — All Years

Product Design

Product Design at DIAT equips every student with Computer-Aided Design (CAD) software skills and systematic design methodology before they begin production in any craft discipline. Students learn to conceptualise, visualise, and technically specify their intended artworks digitally — producing detailed CAD drawings and 3D models of pieces before committing to physical production.

This CAD-to-craft approach bridges indigenous tradition with contemporary design technology, producing graduates who can communicate their work professionally to clients, galleries, and global markets — a capability that directly underpins DIAT's commercial art services arm and the international competitiveness of its graduates.

The Six Craft Disciplines

Specialise in Two of Six Indigenous Crafts

🏺

Clay & Earthenware

One of DIAT's most ancient disciplines, Clay & Earthenware teaches students the full arc of ceramic production — from raw clay preparation and hand-building techniques to wheel-throwing, kiln firing, and surface decoration informed by Ghanaian pottery traditions. Students learn to produce functional vessels, sculptural works, and architectural ceramics.

Read More  →
🧵

Fibres & Fabrics

Fibres & Fabrics explores Ghana's rich textile heritage — from hand-weaving and batik dyeing to macramé, tapestry, and contemporary fibre art. Students study indigenous Kente and adinkra cloth traditions alongside modern textile design methods, developing the ability to produce both traditional and experimental fabric works for cultural, commercial, and gallery contexts.

Read More  →
👜

Leather Technology

Leather Technology covers the full lifecycle of leather craft — from tanning and preparation through pattern-making, cutting, stitching, and finishing. Students learn to produce bags, footwear, accessories, and bespoke leather goods, drawing on West African leather-working traditions while applying contemporary product design principles to create market-ready, professionally finished pieces.

Read More  →
⚙

Metal Production

Metal Production teaches students the traditional and contemporary techniques of metalworking — casting, forging, welding, fabrication, and surface treatment. Grounded in Ghana's goldsmithing and blacksmithing heritage, students learn to produce jewellery, sculpture, functional objects, and architectural metalwork, connecting Ashanti goldcraft tradition with modern fabrication technology.

Read More  →
🍋

Rattan & Bamboo

Rattan & Bamboo is one of DIAT's most environmentally forward disciplines, teaching students to work with these sustainable natural materials to produce furniture, decorative objects, shelving, and architectural installations. Students learn traditional weaving and binding techniques alongside contemporary structural design, producing works that are both ecologically responsible and commercially compelling.

Read More  →
🪓

Wood & Furniture

Wood & Furniture is among DIAT's most commercially active disciplines, training students in joinery, carving, turning, and furniture-making informed by Ghana's rich tradition of carved stools, doors, and ceremonial objects. Students develop technical skills in both hand-tool craft and machine-assisted production, graduating with the ability to design and produce custom furniture, sculpture, and decorative woodwork for residential and commercial clients.

What You Gain

Graduate Outcomes

DIAT graduates are distinctive in the Ghanaian creative economy because they combine two capabilities that most practitioners develop separately: deep indigenous craft expertise and modern design professionalism. The following outcomes describe what every DIAT graduate carries into their career.

Apply / Enquire Now
1

Proficiency in CAD software for designing and specifying craft products before physical production

2

Mastery of two indigenous craft disciplines to professional and commercial production standard

3

Understanding of Ghanaian and West African material culture, aesthetics, and craft heritage

4

Practical experience through the three-month nationwide community internship across Ghana

5

Portfolio of original works across both specialisation disciplines and the Product Design strand

6

Business and commercial awareness for operating as an independent craft practitioner or studio
