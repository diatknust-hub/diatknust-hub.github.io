🏺 B.A. (IRAI) Specialisation

Clay & Earthenware

From raw clay preparation through wheel-throwing, kiln firing, and surface decoration — informed by Ghanaian pottery traditions that stretch back millennia. The discipline bridges ancient form-making with contemporary ceramic art and design.

4 Years B.A. Programme
Curriculum

Skills & Techniques

Core skills developed across the four-year programme, building progressively from foundational technique to advanced, professional-level practice.

01

Hand-building Techniques

Pinch, coil, and slab methods for functional and sculptural ceramic works at all scales.

02

Wheel-throwing

Centring, opening, pulling, and shaping on the potter's wheel — from small vessels to large architectural forms.

03

Kiln Firing

Electric, gas, and traditional wood-fired kiln operation, including temperature management and glaze chemistry.

04

Surface Decoration

Slip decoration, burnishing, resist techniques, and surface texture methods connecting to Ghanaian ceramic traditions.

05

Glaze Development

Understanding glaze chemistry, mixing, testing, and developing glazes from local Ghanaian raw materials.

06

Studio Management

Running a professional ceramic studio — tool maintenance, health and safety, batch production, and client work.

Careers

Where This Takes You

DIAT graduates specialising in Clay & Earthenware enter a wide range of creative, commercial, and academic careers — equipped with both deep craft expertise and modern design professionalism.

🏺

Studio Potter

Running an independent ceramic studio producing functional, decorative, and commissioned ceramic works.

🎓

Ceramic Arts Educator

Teaching ceramic arts at secondary schools, polytechnics, or universities across Ghana.

🏮

Cultural Heritage Specialist

Working with museums, cultural institutions, and archives to document and preserve Ghana's ceramic traditions.

🌌

Craft Export Entrepreneur

Producing and exporting market-ready ceramic goods to international buyers and craft galleries.
