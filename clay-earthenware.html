Clay & Earthenware

Where Earth Becomes Art

One of DIAT's most ancient disciplines, Clay & Earthenware teaches students the full arc of ceramic production — from raw clay preparation and hand-building techniques to wheel-throwing, kiln firing, and surface decoration informed by Ghanaian pottery traditions.

Ghana's ceramic heritage spans thousands of years, from functional cooking vessels to ceremonial pots whose forms carry deep cultural meaning. At DIAT, students learn this heritage not as historical study but as living, technically demanding practice — working with local clay bodies, traditional forming methods, and contemporary kiln technology.

What You Will Learn

Skills & Techniques

The following core skills are developed across the four years of the Clay & Earthenware programme, building progressively from foundational technique to advanced, professional-level practice.

  • Hand-building: pinch, coil, and slab techniques for functional and sculptural works
  • Wheel-throwing: centring, opening, pulling, and shaping on the potter's wheel
  • Clay preparation: wedging, reclaiming, and testing local clay bodies
  • Surface decoration: slip painting, sgraffito, carving, and application of traditional Ghanaian motifs
  • Kiln firing: electric, gas, and traditional pit-firing methods for varying effects
  • Glazing: formulating and applying ceramic glazes for functional and decorative purposes

What Graduates Achieve

Graduate Outcomes

By the end of the Clay & Earthenware specialisation at DIAT, graduates are equipped with the following demonstrable competencies — verified through portfolio assessment, studio examination, and the community internship placement.
