🧵 B.A. (IRAI) Specialisation

Fibres & Fabrics

Kente weaving, adinkra printing, batik, macramé, and contemporary fibre art rooted in Ghana's extraordinary textile heritage — one of the richest in West Africa. Students work with both traditional loom-based techniques and contemporary textile design.

4 Years B.A. Programme
Curriculum

Skills & Techniques

Core skills developed across the four-year programme, building progressively from foundational technique to advanced, professional-level practice.

01

Hand-weaving (Kente)

Strip weaving and broadloom techniques for authentic Kente cloth and contemporary woven textiles.

02

Batik Dyeing

Wax-resist and tie-dye processes using natural and synthetic dyes — from traditional to contemporary applications.

03

Adinkra Printing

Block-printing with carved adinkra symbols — a distinctly Ashanti art form with communicative and ceremonial purpose.

04

Macramé & Knotting

Plant fibre knotting techniques for decorative and functional objects, wall hangings, and fashion accessories.

05

Pattern & Colour Theory

Designing textile patterns, understanding colour harmonies, and translating visual designs to woven or printed cloth.

06

Commercial Textile Production

Calculating material costs, scaling production, and preparing textiles for gallery, fashion, and market contexts.

Careers

Where This Takes You

DIAT graduates specialising in Fibres & Fabrics enter a wide range of creative, commercial, and academic careers — equipped with both deep craft expertise and modern design professionalism.

🧵

Textile Designer

Designing woven, printed, or mixed-media textiles for fashion, interior, and product design markets.

🎓

Weaving Arts Educator

Teaching weaving and textile arts at secondary and tertiary institutions in Ghana and beyond.

🍇

Heritage Textile Specialist

Documenting and preserving traditional Kente and adinkra knowledge for cultural institutions.

🌌

Textile Export Studio

Producing authentic Ghanaian woven textiles for international buyers, galleries, and collectors.
