Who We Are

A Department Unlike Any Other in Ghana

Founded in 1976 as a Diploma programme, the then Department of Integrated Rural Art and Industry (IRAI) — now the Department of Indigenous Art and Technology (DIAT) at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) — is Ghana's pioneering institution for indigenous art education, uniquely combining Academia, commercial craft production, and active Community engagement under one roof.

Operating within the College of Art and Built Environment (CABE) at KNUST, DIAT has for five decades served as the foremost academic custodian of Ghana's indigenous craft traditions — from clay and earthenware to leather, metal, rattan, bamboo, fibres, fabrics, and wood. The department's mandate is not merely to preserve these traditions but to innovate within them, equipping each generation of practitioners with both the cultural knowledge and the contemporary design tools needed to carry indigenous craft into the global marketplace.

Our seven programmes ground every student in Product Design thinking from Year 1, equipping them with Computer-Aided Design (CAD) tools and design methodology before guiding them into mastery of two of six indigenous craft disciplines. This CAD-to-craft approach — designing digitally before making by hand — is what makes DIAT graduates uniquely competitive: they are simultaneously cultural custodians and contemporary design professionals.

How We Work

The DIAT Tripartite Model

DIAT operates across three interconnected arms — not as separate functions but as a unified, mutually reinforcing model that distinguishes the department from every other arts institution in Ghana.

🎓
First Arm

Academic Faculty

Seven accredited programmes grounding students in Product Design and CAD methodology from Year 1, before guiding them into mastery of two of six indigenous craft disciplines. Graduates emerge as both cultural custodians and competitive design professionals equipped for the global creative economy.

🏺
Second Arm

Commercial Art Services

DIAT operates an active art consultancy and commissioned works service, producing bespoke installations, furniture, textiles, and craft pieces for clients across Ghana and beyond. This commercial arm generates Internally Generated Funds (IGF) for KNUST while providing students with real-world professional practice and creating sustainable livelihoods for indigenous craft practitioners.

🌿
Third Arm

Community Engagement

Through a structured three-month internship programme supported by the Mastercard Foundation, DIAT students are embedded with artisan communities across Ghana Region. This arm ensures that the department's academic work remains rooted in living indigenous practice — and that community craft knowledge is formally acknowledged, documented, and attributed.

Celebrating a Half-Century of Indigenous Art

DIAT@50
Est. 1976

Fifty years ago, a small Diploma programme in Kumasi set out to do something radical for its time: to treat Ghana's indigenous craft traditions not as relics of the past but as living disciplines worthy of the highest academic investment. Today, as we mark half a century of that commitment, DIAT's artworks are visible to the world for the first time — and the next fifty years begin.
