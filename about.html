Who We Are

A Department Unlike Any Other in Ghana

Founded in 1976 as the Institute of Rural Art and Industry (IRAI), DIAT is the only university department in Ghana dedicated entirely to indigenous craft disciplines — taught not as cultural history but as living, professionally viable creative practices.

Every student learns Computer-Aided Design before touching clay, metal, leather, or wood. That CAD-to-craft approach — designing digitally, making by hand — bridges ancient Ghanaian tradition with the demands of contemporary global markets.

Our History

Fifty Years of Indigenous Art Education

DIAT’s story is a story of Ghana’s belief in its own creative heritage. From a rural arts institute in 1976 to a fully accredited university department within KNUST’s College of Art and Built Environment, the department has grown into a national centre for indigenous craft excellence — training hundreds of graduates who have gone on to found studios, teach, and represent Ghana’s craft identity globally.

1976

IRAI Founded

The Institute of Rural Art and Industry established at KNUST under the College of Art.

1990

Established Integration Concept

IRAI established its integration concept, bridging traditional craft disciplines with contemporary academic practice.

1999

Diploma to a Degree Program

IRAI programmes upgraded from diploma to full degree status, elevating the academic standing of indigenous art education at KNUST.

2011

Master's Programmes Established

Postgraduate MPhil programmes launched, cementing DIAT's role as a centre of advanced research in indigenous art and technology.

2022

IRAI Rebrands to DIAT

Department officially renamed to the Department of Indigenous Art and Technology, reflecting a broadened scope integrating digital and contemporary tools.

2026 & Beyond

DIAT@50 Celebrations

Half a century of indigenous art education, marked by this digital platform, WebAR experiences, and a year of celebratory events at KNUST.
